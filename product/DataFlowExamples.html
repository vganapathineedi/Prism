Prism Data Flow Examples

How information transforms from sources to employee consumption

1
Email from VP → Prioritized Work Item
SOURCE: EMAIL
Email Arrives
Subject: Q4 Retention Strategy - Need Your Input
From: Sarah Chen (VP Product) To: john@company.com Time: Monday 9:15 AM "Hi John, can you review our Q4 retention proposal and send feedback by Friday EOD? This is critical for the board meeting. Thanks!"
↓
PROCESSING: AGGREGATION
NLP Detection & Parsing
System detects task signals in email using natural language processing:
Detected Task: "Review Q4 retention proposal"
Action Type: Review/Feedback
Deadline: Friday 5:00 PM (explicit)
Requester: Sarah Chen (VP Product - high authority)
Priority Signal: "critical for board meeting"
Estimated Effort: 2-3 hours (based on task type)
↓
PROCESSING: PRIORITIZATION
Multi-Factor Scoring
Prioritization engine evaluates multiple factors:
DEADLINE FACTOR: 9/10 (due Friday, 4 days away) AUTHORITY FACTOR: 10/10 (VP-level request) BUSINESS IMPACT: 9/10 (board meeting, retention strategy) CAPACITY CHECK: 8/10 (3 hours available this week) BLOCKER STATUS: 0/10 (no one waiting on this) ORGANIZATIONAL PRIORITY: 7/10 (retention is Q4 focus) COMPOSITE SCORE: 8.8/10 → CRITICAL PRIORITY
↓
PROCESSING: CAPACITY ANALYSIS
Workload Assessment
System checks John's availability:
Sprint Commitment This Week: 20 hours
Current Non-Project Work: 6 hours (other ad-hoc items)
Available Hours This Week: 40 - 26 = 14 hours
This Task Effort: 3 hours
Utilization After Task: 29/40 = 72.5% (healthy)
Recommended Start: Wednesday morning (allows buffer before Friday deadline)
↓
STORAGE
Work Item Created & Stored
System creates work item with full context:
ID: WI-2847 Title: Review Q4 Retention Proposal Source: Email (sarah.chen@company.com) Deadline: Friday 5:00 PM Priority Level: CRITICAL Effort Estimate: 3 hours Manager: Direct from VP Status: Pending Related: Board meeting, Q4 retention initiative

Key Insights from These Examples
  • Multi-Source Clarity: Information from email, Slack, calendars, and managers automatically flows into one prioritized queue. No context-switching between tools.
  • Intelligent Synthesis: System doesn't just collect—it interprets deadlines, authority levels, business impact, and capacity constraints to create meaningful prioritization.
  • Confidence in Commitments: By seeing full capacity visibility, John can confidently say "yes" to manager requests without overcommitting. He can see where new work fits.
  • Strategic Alignment: Ad-hoc work (response times, reviews) is automatically connected to organizational strategy (customer success). John sees why his work matters.
  • Time Optimization: Rather than "I'm busy," John gets "here's your optimized week: retention review Wed, customer health Thu, support improvements Thu-Fri, sprint work distributed."
  • Manager Efficiency: Managers don't need to manually track who's doing what. They see the team is aligned, has capacity for new initiatives, and understands priorities.
  • Reduced Meetings: By the time standup happens, everyone has already seen their priorities in Prism. Standup is about exception handling, not priority broadcasting.
  • Continuous Improvement: Every override, late completion, or early finish teaches the system. Next week's recommendations are more accurate.
