# Prism Pitch Guide

How to pitch Prism effectively to different audiences with talking points, objection handling, and positioning strategies.

---

## 🎯 Core Pitch Framework

### **The Problem We Solve**
Employees spend 5-20% of their time just figuring out what to work on next. Email, Slack, meetings, manager expectations, org priorities—all fragmented. They're constantly context-switching, missing informal commitments, and making sub-optimal decisions.

### **Our Solution**
Prism intelligently aggregates all non-project work signals from existing systems and answers one question: **"What should I work on next?"** with full context: deadlines, organizational priorities, capacity, and dependencies.

### **The Value**
- **For employees:** 10-15 hours/week saved + 20-30% fewer missed commitments
- **For managers:** 30-50% less status meetings + visibility into team capacity
- **For executives:** Clear alignment between strategy and execution

## 👥 Pitches by Audience

### **For Individual Contributors / Employees**

**Pain Point to Lead With:**
> "How much time do you spend every morning figuring out what to work on? 15 minutes? 30 minutes? And even after that, do you feel confident you're working on the right things?"

**Value Props:**
1. **Clarity** - One prioritized queue instead of checking 5 systems
2. **Confidence** - See full context: deadlines, your capacity, why things are ranked
3. **Focus** - Spend less time deciding, more time doing high-value work
4. **Fair** - System uses objective factors (deadline, authority, impact, capacity) not politics
5. **Early Warning** - System tells you if you're overcommitted BEFORE you commit

**Demo Highlight:**
- Show email arriving
- Show it automatically detected and prioritized
- Show how it fits in their calendar
- Show explanation of why it's prioritized that way
- Show their full week capacity at a glance

**Objection: "Sounds like more overhead"**
> "Actually, it's the opposite. Right now you manually check email, Slack, calendar every morning—that's overhead. Prism removes that overhead by doing the synthesis for you. One login, one unified queue, clear answers."

**Objection: "My manager won't let me use another tool"**
> "Prism doesn't require your manager to do anything different. It reads from email and Slack they're already using. It just makes your job easier by synthesizing what's already coming at you."

---

### **For Managers / Team Leads**

**Pain Point to Lead With:**
> "How much time do you spend in status meetings asking 'what's everyone working on?' And how often are you surprised by a deadline miss because someone was overloaded and didn't tell you?"

**Value Props:**
1. **Visibility** - See team's total workload (sprint + non-project) in real-time
2. **Prevention** - Spot overallocation BEFORE deadlines miss
3. **Efficiency** - Reduce status meetings, team can self-organize
4. **Alignment** - Set priorities once, see them reflected in individual queues
5. **Confidence** - Know whether team can take on new asks

**Demo Highlight:**
- Show team dashboard with each person's capacity
- Show one person at 110% (highlight the issue)
- Show breakdown of their commitments
- Show manager can see what's blocking them
- Show how manager can set a priority and it flows to team's queues

**Objection: "Will this create surveillance culture?"**
> "No. Prism shows capacity and priorities, not detailed task content. You see 'John is at 95% capacity with these priority areas,' not 'John spent 2 hours on X.' It's about preventing overload, not tracking activity."

**Objection: "Teams already use Jira/Asana"**
> "Prism complements those. Your sprint work stays in Jira. Prism handles the non-project work that falls through the cracks—emails, ad-hoc requests, manager priorities, org initiatives. It's the missing piece."

---

### **For Executives / Leadership**

**Pain Point to Lead With:**
> "You announce priorities in all-hands. How confident are you that the organization is actually focused on those priorities? Or is everyone buried in operational work?"

**Value Props:**
1. **Strategic Alignment** - See real-time organizational focus vs. stated strategy
2. **Data-Driven** - Make capacity decisions based on actual workload, not guesses
3. **Efficiency** - Stop repeating priorities in meetings—communicate once
4. **Risk Visibility** - Spot overallocation and burnout patterns early
5. **Competitive Advantage** - Execute strategy faster with aligned effort

**Demo Highlight:**
- Show org dashboard with % of effort on each strategic priority
- Show it's lower than expected (because operations are dominating)
- Show drill-down to see WHY (incidents, unplanned work)
- Show how manager can now redistribute work toward strategic priorities
- Show burnout pattern detection for retention risk

**Objection: "We already have OKRs and a strategy"**
> "Yes, but Prism helps you execute it. You can announce OKRs, but if you can't see whether people are actually focused on them versus buried in operational work, you can't adjust. Prism gives you real-time visibility into execution vs. strategy."

**Objection: "Privacy concerns"**
> "Prism is designed for visibility without surveillance. It shows capacity and priorities at team/org level, not individual task details. And all access is logged and auditable. We take privacy seriously—it's documented in our design principles."

### **For HR / People Operations**

**Pain Point to Lead With:**
> "How many good people are we losing to burnout? And how early could we catch overallocation before it becomes a problem?"

**Value Props:**
1. **Burnout Prevention** - Detect chronic overallocation patterns and intervene early
2. **Retention** - Prevent good employees from burning out by distributing work fairly
3. **Equity** - System detects if some teams/people are consistently overloaded
4. **Mental Health** - Reduce stress by preventing unrealistic commitments
5. **Data for Retention** - Understand if overload is a factor in departures

**Demo Highlight:**
- Show burnout pattern detection (3 weeks at 115%+ capacity)
- Show alerts to manager and HR
- Show how early intervention prevents turnover
- Show equitable workload distribution across teams

**Objection: "This could be used to rate performance"**
> "We're explicit: Prism is not a performance evaluation tool. It's a workload management tool. The data is owned by HR for prevention and intervention, not performance ratings. That's a hard boundary in how we design access controls."

## 🎬 Demo Scripts

### **5-Minute Demo Flow**

**Setup (30 seconds):**
> "Let me show you a typical morning for Jane, a software engineer. She's got sprint work, customer emails, manager priorities, org initiatives—all pulling her attention. Here's what she does today..."

**Step 1: Show the Problem (1 minute)**
- Email arrives: "Can you review the Q4 proposal by Friday?" (VP)
- Slack: "Can you help with this bug?" (colleague)
- Calendar: 4 hours of meetings + sprint work
- Mention of manager priority from yesterday
- Prism before state: Fragmented

**Step 2: Show the Solution (3 minutes)**
- Open Prism
- **"Here's Jane's prioritized work"**
  - "CRITICAL: Review Q4 proposal" (by Friday, from VP, org priority, 3 hours needed)
  - "HIGH: Help with bug" (blocker for others, 4 hours)
  - "MEDIUM: Manager priority—improve API docs" (longer timeline, 5 hours)
- **"Here's why these are ranked"**
  - Show factors: deadline, authority, impact, capacity
- **"Here's her week"**
  - Show calendar with available time
  - Show utilization: 85% (healthy)
  - Show recommended timeline: "Review proposal Wed-Thu, bug fix Friday morning, start API docs next week"
- **"Here's how it connects to company strategy"**
  - Show these tasks link to "Customer Success at Scale" priority

**Outcome (1 minute):**
> "Instead of 30 minutes of manual prioritization, 2 minutes in Prism. Jane knows exactly what to work on, why it matters, and when. Her manager can see she's at healthy capacity. Leadership sees these tasks are moving the needle on company strategy. Everyone wins."

- "Everyone will use this for performance reviews" ❌ (Creates suspicion)
- "This replaces your current tools" ❌ (Creates resistance)
- "This solves all your productivity problems" ❌ (Sets unrealistic expectations)

- "This aggregates signals from tools you already use" ✅
- "This prevents overload by showing capacity" ✅
- "This is for workload management, not performance" ✅
- "This works alongside your current systems" ✅
- "This is one part of better execution" ✅

## 🏆 Competitive Landscape & Positioning

### **Direct Competitors**

#### **Microsoft Loop (Microsoft 365)**
**What it is:** Collaborative work hub within Microsoft 365

**Strengths:**
- Deep Outlook/Teams integration
- Collaboration-first design
- Built into Microsoft ecosystem

**Weaknesses:**
- Not designed for prioritization
- Doesn't manage non-project work
- Treats all items equally

**How to Position Against:**
> "Loop is great for collaboration. Prism is great for prioritization. They're complementary. Loop helps teams work together; Prism helps individuals know what to work on. You can use both."

**What to Say to Loop Users:**
> "You probably use Loop for collaboration. Prism handles what Loop doesn't: intelligent prioritization of your non-project work based on deadlines, capacity, org strategy, and dependencies."

---

#### **Notion / Coda (Work Platforms)**
**What it is:** Flexible database + documents + team management platform

**Strengths:**
- Highly customizable
- Allows many use cases
- Can build custom workflows

**Weaknesses:**
- Requires setup/configuration
- Doesn't auto-aggregate work signals
- Not purpose-built for prioritization
- No learning/AI recommendations

**How to Position Against:**
> "Notion is flexible—you can build almost anything. But prioritization requires structure and data from multiple systems. Prism is purpose-built for that specific problem. It just works without months of setup."

**What to Say to Notion Users:**
> "You've probably tried to build a prioritization system in Notion. It requires constant manual data entry and discipline. Prism does this automatically by reading from your email, Slack, calendar, etc. Same outcome, 10x less work."

---

#### **Slack (Communication Platform)**
**What it is:** Chat/messaging platform where much work happens

**Strengths:**
- Where work signals originate
- Team communication hub
- Deeply embedded in orgs

**Weaknesses:**
- No prioritization logic

**What to Say to Slack Users:**

### **Win-Loss Situations**

**You WIN when:**
- Customer is drowning in non-project work signals across multiple tools
- They've tried to build a solution in Notion/Asana and abandoned it
- They need to cascade org priorities and see if they're sticking

## 💡 Pro Tips

1. **Always Start With Their Pain** - Lead with their specific problem, not your solution
2. **Show, Don't Tell** - A 2-minute demo beats a 10-minute explanation
3. **Use Their Language** - If they say "overload," use "overload." If they say "capacity," use "capacity."
4. **Answer Objections Without Defensiveness** - "That's a great point, here's how we think about it..."
5. **End With A Question** - "Does this address your concern?" vs. "Here's why you're wrong"
6. **Follow Up With Docs** - Send README + relevant architecture doc after pitch
7. **Get Them to Use It** - Pilot > Pitch. A 30-minute experience beats an hour of talking
